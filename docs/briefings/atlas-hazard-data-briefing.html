Update 2026-10-22 — what we have, and what we don't

Have (public, CC-BY-4.0, pinned in §7):

  • Hazard frequency × exposure tables at GAUL 2024 admin 0/1/2, for 33 crops and 5 livestock species × highland/tropical. They cover:
    • 5 scenarios (historic 1995-2014; SSP1-2.6, 2-4.5, 3-7.0, 5-8.5 × four 20-year windows);
    • the 18-GCM NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 ensemble mean;
    • 3 severity tiers.
  • Five exposure variables: production prod_t (t), harvested area harv-area_ha (ha), livestock head_n (head), and value in constant 2014-16 I$ (vop_intld15-21) and nominal 2021 USD (vop_nominal-usd21).
  • Two season windows: jagermeyr (the GGCMI rainfed-maize season) and annual (12 months from the maize planting month).
  • The tonnes tier (prod_t) is the natural input for your formula. value(class) / (value(any) + value(none)) is a production-weighted P per crop and admin unit (§1.7), and any + none is the unit's baseline production in the same table.

Which rows to use:

  • Perennials (coffee, cocoa, tea, banana, plantain, oil palm, coconut, cassava, sugarcane): use period=annual. The maize window badly distorts them; for example, cocoa's crop-specific dry is 94 % on jagermeyr against 7.5 % on annual.
  • Annual crops: use jagermeyr. On annual, a 12-month mean of water-stress days takes in the dry season (maize severe dry 0.59 vs 0.26).
  • Hazard set: NDWS+NTx35+NDWL0 (generic thresholds) is the most stable. Treat PTOT-L+NTxM+PTOT-G as prototype: its rainfall thresholds are crop-cycle totals compared with a maize-window sum (#46).

Have, but outside the hazard tables (observed, display only):

  • CHIRPS v3 / CHIRTS-ERA5 monthly rainfall and temperature (Africa, 0.05°, 1981-2026);
  • SPEI-3 and SPEI-12;
  • MODIS NDVI seasonal means (East Africa);
  • FEWS rangeland WRSI (Kenya).

Don't have:

  • Published per-pixel frequency rasters, or year-by-year hazard series. Both are server intermediates only, and the year-by-year combination classes are not kept at all.
  • Percentile-based severity. All thresholds are fixed absolute values.
  • Uncertainty in the public tables. They are ensemble mean only; the SD and per-GCM values are unpublished.
  • A credible livestock drought layer. It is the crop water-stress index, which saturates in drylands (§6). There is also no forage or biomass anomaly, no water-point access and no grazing mask.
  • Perennial-specific hazards: dry-season length, multi-year deficits, heat at flowering, frost. Perennial heat is decided by the threshold family: generic 35 °C gives coffee, cocoa and tea ~0 %, while EcoCrop's optimum maximum gives robusta 63 %.
  • Hazard rows for the MapSPAM composite groups (fruit, vegetables, other cereals and so on): about 19 % of constant-dollar crop value.
  • Hazards computed on observed CHIRPS / CHIRTS. A streamlined observed-baseline pipeline is being scoped (PLAN_obs_hazard_pipeline.md), about 4 weeks to a first crops version.
  • Livestock nominal-USD values: fixed 2026-10-23 (#47). The vop_nominal-usd21 tiers were priced at the world median and are now republished at national producer prices: +4.6 % continental, 0.43-2.2x per country-species. No workaround is needed. Method: docs/methods/nominal_price_method.md, livestock section.

0. What you need to know first

  1. The Atlas does not publish a per-pixel frequency surface. What is public is a table of hazard frequency × exposure, summed to GAUL 2024 admin units (§1.6). A usable P(hazard, severity) per commodity per admin unit can be recovered exactly from it as a ratio (§1.7). The per-pixel frequency rasters exist only on the processing server.
  2. Severity classes are fixed absolute thresholds, not percentiles (§1.2). Nothing moves with the reference period, so a change in P between periods is a real change in exceedance, not an artefact of the class definition.
  3. Severity classes are cumulative, not bands. "Moderate" means at least moderate, so it contains every severe and extreme season. Summing across severities double-counts (§1.4).
  4. Interaction classes are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. Each season falls into exactly one of: none, dry, heat, wet, dry+heat, dry+wet, heat+wet, dry+heat+wet (§2). This is the Atlas's whole treatment of co-occurrence, and it is the piece most worth inheriting.
  5. One crop calendar is used for everything. The season window is the GGCMI rainfed-maize window (jagermeyr), or 12 months from the maize planting month (annual). Both are applied to every crop and livestock species, and nothing chooses between them per crop. Use the rule in the update section above (§3.2).
  6. The livestock drought layer is the generic crop soil-water-stress index, with the crop thresholds and the maize season window (§4.4). Your concern about it is correct, and in two respects the situation is worse than you describe (§6.1).
  7. Pin what you use. The full rebuild (issue #13) published on 2026-10-16/19. It renamed one hazard set and added one (§1.8). Keys are overwritten in place on republication, so pin the S3 versions in §7 for anything you report.

1. Hazard frequency

1.1 Climate inputs

  • Source: NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 daily data. The indices are computed by the upstream half of the pipeline (hazards_upstream/R/04_indices/) from daily pr, tasmax, tasmin, rsds and hurs (for example hazards_upstream/R/04_indices/fast_calc_NDWS.R:207-211; calc_THI.R:47-50). They are written as monthly rasters: 240 files per hazard per 20-year window (R/1_make_timeseries.R:384-396).
  • Grid: 0.25° (metadata/base_rast_nexgddp.tif: 1440 × 400 cells at 0.25°).
  • Scenarios: historic and ssp126, ssp245, ssp370, ssp585.
  • Periods: historic = 1995-2014, plus 2021-2040, 2041-2060, 2061-2080 and 2081-2100 (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:111-116).
  • The baseline is 1995-2014, the end of the CMIP6 historical run. It is not a WMO 1991-2020 normal (metadata/cdh/africa-hazard-exposure-nexgddp.yaml, note, "BASELINE").
  • Ensemble: 18 GCMs. The membership is stamped into each published tier's JSON sidecar (<parquet key>.json, block ensemble). As read on 2026-10-12 it is: ACCESS-CM2, ACCESS-ESM1-5, CanESM5, CMCC-ESM2, EC-Earth3, EC-Earth3-Veg-LR, GFDL-ESM4, INM-CM4-8, INM-CM5-0, IPSL-CM6A-LR, KACE-1-0-G, MIROC6, MPI-ESM1-2-HR, MPI-ESM1-2-LR, MRI-ESM2-0, NorESM2-LM, NorESM2-MM, TaiESM1. The publisher refuses to ship any membership other than 18 (scripts/r3_publish_tiers.R:347-348). Membership has varied between products in the past (issue #26), so read the sidecar rather than assuming it.

metadata/haz_metadata.csv still describes the indices as computed from CHIRPS, CHIRTS and AgERA5. Those descriptions belong to the observational pipeline. The NEX-GDDP product uses the NEX-GDDP inputs listed above.

1.2 How a hazard season is defined

The chain has three steps.

  1. Seasonal aggregation (R/haz_functions.R:1296-1460, function hazard_stacker, called from R/1_make_timeseries.R:561).
    • Each monthly index is reduced over the season window (§3.2) with the summary function listed in the function column of metadata/haz_metadata.csv (R/haz_functions.R:1360, 1420-1434).
    • This gives one value per pixel per season. A 20-year window yields 19 seasons, because the last season would run past the end of the data (R/haz_functions.R:1249-1252, 1411).
  2. Classification against a threshold. Each season becomes a 0/1 value (R/haz_functions.R:1207-1219; called at R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:862-868).
    • The comparison is strict: > for "greater" hazards and < for "less" hazards.
  3. Frequency. The mean of the 0/1 values over the 19 seasons, per GCM (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:923), then the mean across the 18 GCMs (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:997).

What the summary function does to the units matters. The seasonal value of a day-count index is the mean of its monthly counts over the season months, not a season total:

index seasonal function unit after aggregation meaning of the seasonal value
NDWS (water-stress days) mean days per month mean monthly number of days with AET/PET < 0.5 in the season months
NDWL0 (waterlogging days) mean days per month mean monthly number of waterlogging days
NTx35, NTx40, crop NTx* mean days per month mean monthly number of days with Tmax above the threshold
PTOT sum mm per season seasonal precipitation total
THI_max max °F-scale THI hottest day's THI in the season (see §4.3 caveat)
TAI mean, annual only unitless Thornthwaite aridity index (not windowed, R/haz_functions.R:1374-1384)

Sources: metadata/haz_metadata.csv (function and base_unit columns), R/haz_functions.R:1360.

So the generic severe heat threshold "NTx35 > 14 days" means more than 14 days above 35 °C per month, averaged over the season months. It does not mean more than 14 such days in the season. The generated threshold table (metadata/published_thresholds.md) prints only "day", and the Atlas front-end has quoted a different number for the same threshold (issue #20). Read the unit as "days per month".

1.3 Severity thresholds

All thresholds are fixed absolute values. No percentile, anomaly or reference-period quantity enters the classification anywhere (R/haz_functions.R:1207-1219 compares the seasonal value to a constant). There are two sources of thresholds.

(a) Generic thresholds, from metadata/haz_classes.csv, also rendered in metadata/published_thresholds.md:

hazard index moderate severe extreme
dry NDWS > 15 > 20 > 25 (days per month)
dry PTOT (low) < 500 < 400 < 300 (mm per season)
wet NDWL0 > 2 > 5 > 8 (days per month)
wet PTOT (high) > 1200 > 1600 > 1800 (mm per season)
heat NTx35 > 7 > 14 > 21 (days per month)
heat NTx40 > 1 > 5 > 10 (days per month)

Livestock heat thresholds are species- and zone-specific THI values (§4.3).

(b) Crop-specific thresholds, derived at run time from FAO EcoCrop (metadata/ecocrop.csv) for each MapSPAM commodity (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:153-284):

index moderate severe extreme
PTOT low < Rain_Opt_Min < mean(Rain_Abs_Min, Rain_Opt_Min) < Rain_Abs_Min
PTOT high > Rain_Opt_Max > ceil(mean(Rain_Opt_Max, Rain_Abs_Max)) > Rain_Abs_Max
NTxM (days above Temp_Opt_Max) > 7 > 14 > 21
NTxS (days above ceil(mean(Temp_Opt_Max, Temp_Abs_Max))) > 7 > 14 > 21
NTxE (days above Temp_Abs_Max) > 1 > 5 > 10

For maize this gives NTxM = 33 °C, NTxS = 40 °C, NTxE = 47 °C, and PTOT low thresholds of 600 / 500 / 400 mm. Where one MapSPAM commodity maps to several species, the thresholds are averaged (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:277-281).

The crop-specific rainfall classes use the wrong window. EcoCrop's rainfall limits are crop-cycle totals: FAO labels the field "annual", but the reference implementation (dismo::ecocrop) compares them with rainfall summed over the crop's own cycle, (GMIN + GMAX) / 2 days. The pipeline compares them with PTOT summed over the rainfed-maize window for every crop (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:175-198).

  • Measured on 1995-2015 CHIRPS, the effect is large. Severe dry fires in 90 % of seasons for cocoa and 94 % for plantain on the maize window, against 32 % and 37 % on their own cycle. The live product shows the same: cocoa 94 %, plantain 96 %.
  • Short-cycle annuals move the other way.
  • Wheat, barley, rice and sugarcane stay near 80-90 % on any window. That points at the thresholds and at irrigated production being included.

Treat the crop-specific rainfall sets as prototype. Tracked as issue #46, with the measurements.

1.4 Severity classes are cumulative, not bands

Each tier is classified independently against its own threshold. Because the thresholds are monotone (moderate < severe < extreme for > hazards, and the reverse for <), a season that exceeds "extreme" also exceeds "severe" and "moderate". For one hazard:

P(≥ moderate) ≥ P(≥ severe) ≥ P(≥ extreme)

Band probabilities follow by differencing, for single-hazard marginals only:

P(moderate band) = P(≥ moderate) − P(≥ severe)

For an avoided-loss sum, an equivalent formulation needs no differencing:

Σ_s P(≥ s) · [L(s) − L(s−1)],   where L(s) is the loss fraction at severity s and L(0) = 0

Over a single hazard this is exactly Σ over bands of P(band) · L(band). It does not carry over cleanly to the interaction classes; see §2.3.

1.7 Recovering P(hazard, severity) per commodity — the route that works today

Because value is Σ_p f_p·V_p over the pixels p of a unit, and any + none = Σ_p V_p, the ratio below is the value-of-production-weighted mean frequency of the class within the unit:

P_u(class, sev) = value(class) / [ value(any) + value(none) ]

taken for one crop, hazard_vars, scenario, timeframe, severity and admin unit.

I verified the identities on the live severe tier for Kenya (admin-0, historic and SSP5-8.5 2041-2060):

  • the seven exclusive classes sum to any to within the 3-decimal rounding of the frequencies (for example maize, NDWS+NTx35+NDWL0, historic: 466.60 M against 466.38 M);
  • any + none is constant across scenarios for a given crop and set (1,117.78 M against 1,117.82 M).

Three consequences:

  • Weighting by value is the right weighting for your formula. Σ_u P_u × production_u reproduces the pixel-level Σ f_p × production_p, provided production is distributed within the unit like value (it is, at fixed prices). For a different weighting (area, or another production map) you need the rasters.
  • NaN rows: about 28 % of admin-2 units are NaN in every class. These are most likely the units the 0.25° grid cannot resolve: when zones are rasterised, each cell takes a single zone, and these units end up with none. Measured on the prod_t severe jagermeyr tier for maize, 1,232 of 4,342 units. Drop them (NOT isnan(value)) and use the admin-1 row for those areas. Units with zero production give 0 / 0, so guard the division.
  • Integer rounding. R/3 rounds each pixel's product to a whole unit before summing. On the prod_t tier, after dropping NaN rows, the seven classes match any within 1 % in all but 67 of 2,894 maize units (11 beyond 5 %, all small producers). The continental error is 0.02 %.
  • Divide within the same hazard_vars set. Total value differs slightly between sets (maize, Kenya: 1,117.8 M for NDWS+NTx35+NDWL0 against 1,145.5 M for PTOT-L+NTxS+PTOT-G), because the indices have different no-data masks.
  • Units with tiny exposure give unstable ratios. The 0.25° grid cannot resolve about 2,100 small admin-2 units that the 0.05° exposure grid can (CDH record note). Use admin-1 or admin-0 where admin-2 exposure is small.

Baseline production for the same units, on the same grid, is in the exposure denominator crop-livestock_all_res-25.parquet (§7). Its rows with unit = t are MapSPAM 2020 production in tonnes, and its rows with unit = ha are harvested area. Select on unit: the table holds five units.

2. Interactions (co-occurring hazards)

2.1 Which interactions exist

There is one interaction structure: a three-way dry × heat × wet classification. Each published hazard_vars value names the three indices, in the order dry + heat + wet (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:402-421, 573-577):

hazard_vars dry heat wet applies to slots fixed or crop-specific
NDWS+NTx35+NDWL0 NDWS NTx35 NDWL0 all crops all generic; computed once and applied to every crop
PTOT-L+NTxS+PTOT-G (live; being renamed …NTxM…) PTOT low crop NTx PTOT high each crop all crop-specific
NDWS+NTxM+NDWL0 (after #13) NDWS crop NTx NDWL0 each crop heat crop-specific, dry and wet generic
NDWS+THI-max+NDWL0 NDWS THI_max NDWL0 livestock heat species-specific, dry and wet generic
PTOT-L+THI-max+PTOT-G PTOT low THI_max PTOT high livestock heat species-specific, PTOT generic

Dry × heat, dry × wet, heat × wet and the triple are all classes within this one structure. No other interaction is computed: HSH, TAVG, NTx40, TAI and cold are not in any set (HSH: see archive/dispatches/HANDOVER_2026-10-07.md, "B7").

2.2 How the classes are built

For each GCM and each season (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:1574-1615):

  1. The three 0/1 seasonal classifications at one severity are encoded as dry × 1 + heat × 10 + wet × 100. Each season therefore gets a code in {0, 1, 10, 100, 11, 101, 110, 111}.
  2. For each code, a layer is 1 where the season has exactly that code and 0 otherwise (int_risk, R/haz_functions.R:1236-1242, which masks every other code to 0). That layer is averaged over the 19 seasons.
  3. any = the mean of (code ≥ 1), and none = 1 − any (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:1600-1610).
  4. Class frequencies are then averaged across the 18 GCMs (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:1679). A mean of partitions is still a partition, so the ensemble classes still sum to 1.

So:

  • The classes are mutually exclusive and exhaustive. dry means dry only: dry ≥ threshold and heat and wet both below theirs. The seven non-empty classes sum to any.
  • Joint probabilities are empirical, counted season by season within each GCM. Any dependence between the hazards that the GCM produces (dry seasons being hot, for example) is carried through. No correlation, copula or independence assumption is made or published.
  • All three hazards are classified at the same severity. There is no "severe dry with moderate heat" class. A season that is severe-dry and only moderately hot falls in dry in the severe file and in dry+heat in the moderate file.
  • dry+wet is a real, non-empty class. Dryness and wetness are separate indices summarised over the same window, so one season can have high mean stress days and high mean waterlogging days (an early dry spell and a late flood, for instance).

2.3 How interaction layers relate to single-hazard layers, and what double-counts

Single-hazard marginals are sums of classes. At one severity:

P(dry) = P(dry) + P(dry+heat) + P(dry+wet) + P(dry+heat+wet)        [right-hand side: exclusive classes]
P(dry ∩ heat) = P(dry+heat) + P(dry+heat+wet)

Single-hazard exposure is not published separately (R/3_freq_x_exposure.R:1356-1370, "solo hazards … can be constructed from the interactions").

These sums double-count:

summing over why it double-counts
hazards' marginals: P(dry) + P(heat) + P(wet) co-occurring seasons are counted two or three times. The exclusive classes are the correct thing to sum.
severity tiers tiers are cumulative (§1.4)
hazard_vars sets they are alternative definitions of the same three hazards, not additional hazards
any with any class any is the sum of the seven classes
admin levels within one file admin-0 and admin-1 rows are sums of the admin-2 rows
generic-crop with the individual crops generic-crop is all crops' value combined, under the generic set

What we suggest you inherit. Assign a loss fraction and a practice effectiveness to each exclusive class at a chosen severity, and sum over the seven classes:

avoided loss_u = production_u × Σ_{c ∈ 7 classes} P_u(c, s) × L(c, s) × E(practice, c, s)

This cannot double-count. It lets a compound class carry a loss that is not the sum of its parts (L(dry+heat) need not equal L(dry) + L(heat)), which is the point of having the classes. Its limits:

  • Severity must be handled one tier at a time. The incremental formulation in §1.4 is exact only for single-hazard marginals. Interaction classes are not nested across tiers: a season in dry+heat at severe can be dry+heat+wet at moderate. So across tiers you can either
    • run the calculation once per tier and report it as a sensitivity, or
    • use the marginal-increment form for each hazard and accept that the compound effect is then additive. There is no exact class-by-band decomposition without the per-season data, which is not saved (§1.5).
  • No uncertainty is published. Each GCM's frequency is a count over 19 seasons, so it moves in steps of about 0.053. The across-GCM SD exists on the server (ENSEMBLEsd). The planned public value_sd is a sum of per-pixel SDs, not the SD of the aggregate (R/3_freq_x_exposure.R:1613), so it should not be read as an admin-level uncertainty.

3.2 Season windows: one calendar for everything

  • The window comes from GGCMI Phase 3 crop calendar v1.01, rainfed maize (mai_rf_ggcmi_crop_calendar_phase3_v1.01.nc4, R/1_make_timeseries.R:274-286). The planting and maturity day-of-year values are converted to months.
  • The published period=jagermeyr is the window from the maize planting month to the maize maturity month, inclusive, wrapping across the year boundary (R/haz_functions.R:1397-1402, 1417-1434). The name refers to Jägermeyr et al., the authors of the GGCMI calendar.
  • The same window is used for every crop and every livestock species. There is no crop-specific calendar. Coffee, cassava, sugarcane, banana and cattle are all assessed over the rainfed-maize season.
  • One season per year. The second rainy season in bimodal regions (East Africa's short rains, for example) is not represented. A start-of-season route with two seasons exists in code but is disabled for lack of a usable dataset (R/1_make_timeseries.R:245-270, 353-355).
  • period=annual, published since 2026-10-19, is a 12-month window starting at the maize planting month (R/haz_functions.R:1403-1405). It is not a calendar year. Nothing routes perennials to it automatically, so choose it yourself for perennials (#46).
  • So the indices are computed within a growing season, but it is maize's season, not the commodity's.

3.3 Exposure

  • Production data: MapSPAM2020_AdaptationAtlas_SSA (DOI 10.7910/DVN/Z0HK7R, CC-BY-4.0, metadata/cdh/mapspam2020-adaptation-atlas-ssa.yaml). This is the Alliance's SSA release, not IFPRI's global SPAM 2020 v2r2. Technology all (rainfed and irrigated combined) throughout R/3_freq_x_exposure.R (L380, L400, L415, L428).
  • What is multiplied against frequency in the published tier: crop value of production in nominal 2021 USD.
    • The value is MapSPAM production × a FAOSTAT implied farm-gate price, using the median of 2019-2023, allocated and priced on the native 0.05° grid (R/0.4.2_create_crop_vop_nominal_usd.R; method in docs/methods/nominal_price_method.md).
    • It is summed onto the 0.25° hazard grid before the multiplication (R/3_freq_x_exposure.R:133-152).
  • Nominal, not deflated. Do not compare values across periods as real terms. The constant 2014-16 international-dollar tier (vop_intld15-21) is now published (§7).
  • Physical tiers, published. Harvested area (harv-area_ha), production (prod_t) and livestock head (head_n) are crossed with hazard (R/3_freq_x_exposure.R:980-1042). For your formula, use prod_t (§1.7, §7).

4.3 Livestock heat: it is a THI

  • Formula: THI = (1.8·T + 32) − (0.55 − 0.0055·RH)·(1.8·T − 26.8), from Rahimi et al. 2020 (hazards_upstream/R/04_indices/calc_THI.R:59).
    • T is daily Tmax (tasmax). RH is daily mean relative humidity (hurs) (L47-50).
    • Daily-mean RH paired with Tmax overstates THI on the hottest hour. The Atlas's human heat index corrects RH to its value at Tmax; THI does not.
    • THI_mean is also computed from Tmax (L65), not from mean temperature. metadata/THI_thresholds_provenance.md:15-17 says otherwise and is wrong on this point.
  • Monthly products: the monthly maximum and the monthly mean of daily THI (L65-66).
  • Seasonal value: THI_max uses max, so it is the season's highest daily THI.
  • Thresholds: species × zone, in °F-scale THI (metadata/haz_classes.csv:44-103). Extreme values are from Thornton et al. 2021, Global Change Biology 27:5762 (Table 2). Moderate and severe are a curated mix, documented in metadata/THI_thresholds_provenance.md.

pigs 75 / 79 / 92 75 / 79 / 89

  • An unresolved provenance gap. The current code does not produce the seasonal NEX-GDDP THI series that the frequency stage reads.
    • R/1_make_timeseries.R:297-298 drops THI and HSH whenever the input path is the NEX-GDDP tree.
    • The THI files on the server come from an earlier run.
    • If that run used hazard_stacker, a long-standing slip at R/haz_functions.R:1344 (max = haz_files_mean) means "THI_max" would be the seasonal max of monthly-mean THI. That would bias it low and understate heat exceedance.

5. Not covered, or not done here

Of the four topics you asked about, these do not exist (publicly, or at all where stated):

  8. A livestock production-system mask. Only highland/tropical exists.
  9. Camels.
  10. A livestock-specific drought index, or livestock-specific drought thresholds.
  11. A forage or biomass anomaly layer of any kind, observed or projected.
  12. Water-point or surface-water access. The only water layer is a static lakes-and-wetlands mask used to exclude water cells (hazards_upstream/R/02_preprocess_data/createAfricaWaterBodiesMask.R).
  13. Population exposure under this product.

6. The livestock drought problem

6.1 Do we agree? Yes, and it is worse than your description in two respects

You describe "drought indices built on crop water stress within a growing season". Mechanically, the livestock drought layer is the following:

  • A generic crop index reused, not even a crop-specific one.
    • NDWS is a single-layer bucket water balance with a Priestley-Taylor-type potential ET, run on NEX-GDDP daily inputs.
    • A day counts as stressed when the bucket's AET/PET ratio is below 0.5 (hazards_upstream/R/04_indices/fast_calc_NDWS.R:22-75, 165-174; kernel wbkernel/src/wb_kernel.cpp:99-147).
    • It has no crop coefficient. Its soil capacity is built to a maize rooting depth, clamped to 45-100 cm (hazards_upstream/R/02_preprocess_data/createAfricaSoils.R:29, 105-122). The link from that script to the exact file NDWS reads is not confirmed in the repository.
  • Integrated over the maize season, at rangeland pixels too.
    • Where there is little or no maize, the GGCMI calendar's maize window still defines the season.
    • The rangeland rainy season, the dry season in which forage runs out, and the year-to-year carry-over are not represented.
  • Classified on crop benchmarks: more than 15, 20 or 25 stress days per month on average.
  • Each season is an independent draw. The bucket carries soil water from month to month (the AVAIL fix, fast_calc_NDWS.R:140-150). However, frequency is the mean of independent per-season 0/1 classes. There is no persistence term, no consecutive-failure class and no multi-season accumulation. The well-documented two-failed-seasons pathway to mortality and distress sales cannot appear.

The alternative set (PTOT-L) is no better. Fixed crop benchmarks saturate in drylands. Measured on the live extreme tier for cattle-tropical, historic period, the marginal probability of the dry class in Kenya's arid counties (the four dry-containing classes ÷ total) is:

county NDWS+THI-max+NDWL0 PTOT-L+THI-max+PTOT-G
Garissa 0.47 0.40
Mandera 0.60 0.75
Marsabit 0.75 0.58
Turkana 0.72 0.43
Wajir 0.83 0.86

So "extreme" drought occurs in 40-86 % of seasons. That is the climatology of a dryland, not a drought signal, and it says little about which seasons are bad for herds.

One correction to your framing. Within the Atlas these rows are not the result of a deliberate "crop index applied to livestock" decision. They are generic rows copied to livestock so that the interaction structure would be complete (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:138-147). Nobody has validated them for livestock. The issue tracking the gap is hazards_prototype #31 (open; the measurements and plan below are posted there): "Rangeland drought: integrate NDVI and pasture biomass to represent forage-based climate impact for livestock". This document's recommendation is consistent with it.

Your point that the error is invisible downstream stands: the layer produces a well-formed probability everywhere.

6.2 Answers to the specific questions

  • What the layer is mechanically: a generic soil-water bucket index (or a seasonal rainfall total), over the rainfed-maize window, on crop thresholds. See §4.4 and §6.1.
  • Does a forage or biomass anomaly layer exist? No. MODIS NDVI seasonal means exist for East Africa only, with no anomaly, and FEWS rangeland WRSI exists for Kenya only (§4.5).
    • Could one be built? Yes, as an observed layer. It cannot be projected. NDVI and biomass have no counterpart in NEX-GDDP, for the same reason the pipeline already excludes the dry-days index NDD from projections (R/2_calculate_haz_freq.R:126).
    • So under future scenarios a forage anomaly can only serve as a calibration and validation target for a meteorological index that is projectable. It cannot be the hazard itself.
  • Should livestock severity use different benchmarks? Yes.
    • A rangeland drought class should be defined on the distribution of the index at each pixel, for example the 1-in-5, 1-in-10 and 1-in-20 dry season or 12-month period of the 1995-2014 baseline, per GCM. That is an anchored percentile, not a rolling one.
    • Baseline P is then fixed by construction (0.2, 0.1, 0.05), and the future periods carry the signal. This changes your method in the way you anticipated: the baseline term becomes a design constant rather than an estimate.
    • Fixed absolute benchmarks transferred from crops have no physiological basis for grazing animals, and saturate in drylands.
    • A consecutive-failure class (two or more failed seasons in a row) belongs alongside them.
  • Is water-point or surface-water access represented? No (§5, item 12). Observed surface-water history exists externally, for example JRC Global Surface Water, but it is not ingested. It is not projectable either, and access depends on infrastructure that no climate layer describes.

6.3 Options

Effort is in working weeks of one analyst, with node time noted separately. These are estimates, not commitments.

Option A — Reframe the existing projectable indices for rangeland. About 4-6 weeks, plus about 2 days of node time.

  • Restrict livestock drought to grazing systems using a production-system mask. The GLPS directory exists but must first be checked for contents.
  • Compute the index over the 12-month window rather than the maize window. The annual period already does this from the maize planting month; a rangeland-specific window would be better.
  • Use a multi-month water-balance or SPEI-type index from NEX-GDDP. calc_PET.R exists upstream.
  • Define severity by baseline-anchored percentiles, and add a consecutive-failure class. The per-season 0/1 series this needs already exists on the server for single hazards; new code is needed for the run-length class.
  • Gives: a coherent, projectable, rangeland-framed meteorological drought index.
  • Does not give: any evidence that it tracks forage.

Option B — Observed forage anomaly, historical only. About 3-4 weeks.

  • Extend the NDVI ingest from Kenya and East Africa to sub-Saharan Africa. The ingest is python/ingest_ndvi_modis.py; it needs NASA Earthdata credentials, and the current default bounding box is Kenya.
  • Compute per-pixel seasonal anomalies and a VCI against a fixed baseline, masked to grazing systems.
  • Candidate biomass products, not yet verified for coverage, latency or licence: Copernicus DMP, FEWS NET rangeland products, and WaPOR, which was considered and deferred in archive/dispatches/NDVI_ingest_plan.md.
  • Gives: a defensible description of past rangeland drought.
  • Gives nothing for future scenarios.

Option C — B as the target for A: a validated projectable proxy. About 3-4 months.

  • Over rangeland pixels for 2000-2024, test which observed meteorological index (SPEI at 3, 6 or 12 months, the 12-month water balance, rainfall percentile, consecutive-failure counts) best predicts the observed forage anomaly classes. Choose the index and thresholds on that evidence, then compute the same index from NEX-GDDP for the projections.
  • Do the validation with observed climate, not NEX-GDDP. A GCM's historical run is not year-synchronous with observed weather, so its 1995-2014 seasons cannot be matched to NDVI seasons. The observational SPEI (CHIRPS v3 + CHIRTS-ERA5, R/observational/2_calculate_obs_spei.R) already exists Africa-wide, so most of the climate side of the validation is built.
  • Gives: a projected livestock drought P, with a stated skill against observed forage, on benchmarks chosen for livestock. This is the defensible product.

Option D — A forage process model driven by GCMs. For example, a rangeland biomass model with grazing pressure.

  • More than six months, plus parameterisation data we do not hold.
  • Not achievable by March 2027.

Not addressable by any of these within the timeframe: water-point access; the impact chain from forage deficit to body condition, milk, conception, mortality and offtake (the loss fraction side, which is yours); and carry-over beyond the persistence a consecutive-failure class captures.

6.4 Recommendation

Do Option C, with Option A as its projectable half, and set a decision gate in mid-January 2027.

  • November to mid-January: the grazing mask, the SSA NDVI anomaly (B), and the validation analysis against observed SPEI and water-balance indices.
  • At the gate:
    • If one index reaches useful skill against forage anomaly classes in rangeland pixels, compute it from NEX-GDDP with baseline-anchored percentile classes plus a consecutive-failure class (A). Run it through the frequency stage, and publish it as a separate livestock drought set by March. This is realistic if the work starts in November and the node is free (a frequency stage takes about a day and a half per period).
    • If no index has skill, report the gap. Publish the observed forage anomaly as a historical layer, and state that no projected livestock drought probability is defensible. We agree with you that this is better than a confident wrong number.
  • Until then, do not use either livestock dry axis in an external deliverable. If a livestock heat-only figure is needed, the livestock heat class is computable, but read §4.3 first.

       value / NULLIF(SUM(value) FILTER (WHERE hazard IN ('any','none'))
                 OVER (PARTITION BY gaul2_code, scenario, timeframe), 0) AS p
FROM t
WHERE hazard NOT IN ('any');                   -- 7 exclusive classes + none; they sum to ~1
